I recently built a face vise for my workbench and wanted to talk about it as I don't see this style of vise very often.
I have been using for a couple months now and I don't see myself going back to the leg vise I used previously.
The face vise, in my opinion, should be more popular.
It is more difficult to build than a leg vise not very much so.
I reckon if you built your bench then you most likely have the skills to build a face vise.

Face vises are usually found on english style benches but I think you could make one for any bench.
It consists of the jaw, the screw, a guide bar, and an internal track for the guide bar.
The guide and track are the most important parts of the construction.
The bar must be perpindicular to the jaw and joined securely to it.
The track must fit such that the bar slides smoothly but has minimal play.
This keeps the jaw parallel to the bench and allows the screw to provide ample clamping.

Using the vise is very simple, only requiring cranking the screw in or out.
There are no other adjustments necessary unlike my leg vise which needed the wedge on the floor be moved to accomodate whatever I was clamping.
It seems like a small improvement but it has definitely made using my bench and building projects more enjoyable now that I can just clamp parts without needing to do that extra step.
It also frees up space around the leg which means I'm not bumping my feet into anything or having to adjust how I'm standing.
The last thing I enjoy is the asthetic of the vise.
The rectangular jaw just seems to look "correct" mounted on my english style bench.
It matches the boxy nature of the rest of the bench well.
It has a simple and traditional look that I appreciate when I'm in the shop.

For reference, my vise is 9"x20".
There's about 7 inches left of the screw and a little over 8 inches between the screw and guide bar.
This allows clamping wide boards vertically with ease.
The vise has about 4 inches of depth from the bench top to the screw which handles most boards I work with easily.
I also planed a slight taper on the inside face of the jaw such that the bottom is approximately 1/8 inch thinner.
When clamping, the top of the jaw contacts first with the rest of the jaw contacting as the screw is tightened.
This prevents the top from opening up due to the slight of amount of wracking inherent in the vise.
The only downside I've noticed so far in comparison with my leg vise is when it comes to edge planing very wide boards (8+ inches).
The leg vise had a depth of 10 inches or so to the screw so even a 12 wide board only sat a couple inches above the bench and could be easily planed.
In the face vise, that would be about 8 inches above the bench which is doable but awkward.
I can clamp to the left of the screw to mitigate this but then longer boards might stick out past the other end of the bench.
This is also doable but potentially awkward.
My bench is only 6ft long so having to shift the workpiece left of the screw means I'm a little more limited.
The depth of the leg vise meant I have the full 6ft of the bench avaiable to clamp to.
I don't think this is going to be a big issue in practice but it is something to be aware of when considering a face vise for your bench.

To conclude my thoughts, the face vise does everything a leg vise can do and is honestly better when it comes to vertical clamping tasks.
You are limited slightly when needing to work on edges of very wide stock but this is not usally an issue in practice.
The face vise is simpler to use but more difficult to build.
This tradeoff is worth it in my opinion.
You build the vise once and a rewarded with easier operation for it's lifetime of usage.
The leg vise, while simpler to build, requires a bit more fiddling to operate.
I used a leg vise for years and was quite happy with it but I was always curious about face vises.
After building and using one for a few months I don't foresee myself going back to the leg vise.
If you're considering building one for your bench, I definetly recommend doing so.
